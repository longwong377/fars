# SOUNDSCAPE (Persepolis and the Marvdasht plain, 467 BCE)

Researched on 2026-09-22. Classical texts were **read in full** from the Perseus Digital Library TEI files on GitHub
(`PerseusDL/canonical-greekLit`). All other items are **web-search extracts**, because the scholarly hosts are blocked.
There is no Persepolis-specific evidence for music. Everything musical is carried over from Greek reports of the Persian
court or from Mesopotamian and Elamite parallels, so it is capped at **B** and is usually **C** for the actual sound design.
For fauna, modern Fars distributions are used as a proxy for 467 BCE (C unless stated).

## 1. Court music and voice

| value | source | tier |
|---|---|---|
| At the king's supper "his concubines sing and play to him; and one of them leads, and then all the rest sing in concert" (Heracleides of Cumae, *Persika*, quoted by Athenaeus, *Deipn.* 4.145c = Perseus book 4 ch. 26, Yonge tr.) | Perseus `tlg0008.tlg001.perseus-eng2.xml`, read | B (4th-c. Greek report about the court in general, not 467 or Persepolis) |
| Same passage: the king usually dines alone or with his wife or sons. Guests "sit on the ground and he reclines on a couch with golden feet". Servers wait "after having bathed, wearing beautiful clothes", "nearly half the day in attendance" | same | B |
| "three hundred women are his guard ... they sleep all day, that they may watch all night; and they pass the whole night in singing and playing, with lights burning" (Heracleides via Athenaeus 12.514b = Perseus book 12 ch. 8) | same | B (night-time court soundscape) |
| Persian sacrifice uses **no music**: "they do not build altars or kindle fire, employ libations, or music, or fillets, or barley meal" (the Greek has *aulos*, 'pipe'). "a Magus comes near and chants over it the song of the birth of the gods ... no sacrifice can be offered without a Magus" (Herodotus 1.132, Godley tr.) | Perseus `tlg0016.tlg001.perseus-eng2.xml`, read | B (Herodotus c. 430 BCE, near-contemporary) |
| → **Design rule:** religious sound = unaccompanied male chant (magus) in the open air; **no pipes, no drums** at sacrifice | inference from the row above | C |
| Achaemenid depictions show five recognisable instruments: "a lute, a double pipe, two vertical harps, a round-bodied lyre, and perhaps small percussion"; hymns were "collectively performed" by "specially trained groups" | Wikipedia "Achaemenid music" (search extract; its underlying source not seen) | B/C |
| Music in state ceremonies, especially religious ones, under the Achaemenids | Iranica "Music history i. Pre-Islamic Iran" (search extract) | B |

## 2. Instruments available in the Achaemenid / Elamite / Mesopotamian world

| value | source | tier |
|---|---|---|
| **Vertical angular harp** and **horizontal angular harp** used in Iran; vertical to horizontal harps in the ratio 4:3 on Elamite reliefs at Madaktu and Kul-e Farah. The same extract dates Kul-e Farah to the "1st century BCE". **That date is wrong or garbled**: Kul-e Farah reliefs are Neo-Elamite. Treat the date as unverified | Wikipedia "Achaemenid music" / Farrokh (search extracts) | B (instrument types), C (date) |
| **Arched harp** in Elamite art from c. 3300–3100 BCE; bull lyres c. 2450 BCE; lutes from c. 1300 BCE "popular with the upper class" | same extract | B |
| **Double pipe** (Greek *aulos*-type), **round-bodied lyre**, **lute**, small percussion: Achaemenid depictions | same | B/C |
| Frame drum, cymbals and clappers: Mesopotamian standards. **No Achaemenid-specific source was seen in this pass** | – | C |
| Flute-accompanied "Persian dance" with clashing shields is described by Xenophon (quoted in Athenaeus, Yonge tr.). It was performed by a non-Persian soldier in 400 BCE, so it is **not** court evidence | Perseus Athenaeus file, read | C (for Persepolis) |
| Military signals: no source was seen. Trumpets are known from Oxus finds (c. 2200–1750 BCE, extract above) but are **not** attested for the Achaemenid army here | Wikipedia extract | C |

## 3. Tuning (for any synthesised harp or lyre)

| value | source | tier |
|---|---|---|
| **CBS 10996** (Neo-Babylonian, c. 700 BCE, Nippur) lists fourteen string-pair intervals on a 7-string (+2 octave) system. Kilmer read it as **heptatonic** (ascending). Dumbrill argues the system is **descending** | musicircle.net / Dumbrill "The case for CBS 10996" (search extracts) | B |
| **UET VII 126** (Ur): string names. Read as evidence for simultaneous **7- and 9-string** (heptatonic / enneatonic) practice | same | B |
| **UET VII 74**: nine strings, string 8 tuned with string 1 and string 9 with string 2 → octaves → a **seven-note** scale | same | B |
| → **Design rule:** tune a 9-string harp or lyre heptatonically, with strings 8–9 an octave above 1–2. Use the Kilmer/Dumbrill mode cycle (*išartum*, *kitmum*, *embūbum*, *pītum*, *nīš gabarîm*, *nīd qablim*, *qablītum*). Mode names come from general knowledge and were **not** seen in extracts | – | C |

## 4. Birds (Marvdasht plain, Kur/Pulvar, Kuh-e Rahmat)

No site-specific ornithological list was retrieved. The species below are attested for Iran or Fars in the extracts. Their
seasonality near Persepolis is inferred (C).

| value | source | tier |
|---|---|---|
| **See-see partridge** (*Ammoperdix griseogularis*): resident "in dry, open and often hilly country". Fits the rocky Kuh-e Rahmat slope behind the terrace. Loud calls at dawn and dusk | Wikipedia "See-see partridge" (search extract) | B (species), C (on-site) |
| **Chukar** (*Alectoris chukar*): on the list of birds of Iran; "partridge" reported in Bamu NP, 10 km NE of Shiraz | Wikipedia "List of birds of Iran"; HiPersia Bamu page (extracts) | B/C |
| **Golden eagle**, **buzzard**: raptors of Bamu NP (Zagros, Fars) | HiPersia / visitthepersia Bamu (extracts) | B/C |
| **European bee-eater** (*Merops apiaster*): summer breeder over western Asia; winters in Africa. Uses river valleys, cultivated plains and riverbanks, i.e. the Pulvar banks in **Apr–Sep** | Birds of the World / BirdForum (extracts) | B (range), C (Marvdasht) |
| **European roller**, **Eurasian hoopoe**: "among the commoner birds" of Iran, in semi-arid steppe. Summer migrants (hoopoe partly resident) | Fatbirder / surfiran (extracts) | C |
| **Wetland birds** of the Kur terminal lakes (Bakhtegan/Tashk, c. 100+ km east): flamingo, crane, pelican, stork, ducks, geese; migrants in winter | Wikipedia "Bakhtegan Lake" (extract) | B (for the lakes); C (overflights at Persepolis) |
| Swallows, swifts, larks, sparrows, doves, ravens/crows: expected but **not sourced** in this pass | – | C |

## 7. Water

| value | source | tier |
|---|---|---|
| **Pulvar** (Sivand): about 150 km long, the main tributary of the Kur. It flows past Naqsh-e Rustam and joins the Kur in the Marvdasht plain. Today it is intermittent and often dry in summer and autumn | LANDSCAPE.md (Wikidata Q5680617; Kor River extracts) | B |
| **Kur** flows to Bakhtegan/Tashk lakes | Wikipedia "Kor River" (extract) | B |
