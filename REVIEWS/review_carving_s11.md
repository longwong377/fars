# Review: D-306 carving (s11-carving): column capitals and the Gate of All Nations' colossi

Reviewer: independent, blind. Brief: `handoff/briefs/s11/review_carving.md`. Date: 2026-09-27.

## Verdict first: what is broken

**Fail on every gate this sample can test. T-A4: the lowest category score is 1 (needs ≥ 4). T-A4cg: 22 of 22 judged views
read as CG (needs 0). T-A5: I picked the photograph in 4 of 4 pairs (100 %), and the pairs were trivial to pick.**

1. **Carving form, capitals (the main failure).** The double-bull protome reads as a toy cow. The body is a smooth, bulbous
   inflated shape. The face is a dog-like muzzle with nostril holes and **no carved eye at all**. The ears are flat leaves and
   the horns are faceted low-poly cones. The references (fallen capital three-quarter, head side-on) show something else: a
   stylised, planar Achaemenid head with a large lidded eye, the scalloped scale-rows of the mane running over the crown and
   neck, the curled forelock, the rosette collar, the tasselled blanket, and folded forelegs with articulated knees and hooves.
   The render's legs are stubby blocks.
2. **Carving form, W bulls (Gate).** From the passage, the bulls sit in a rectangular recess under a plain slab "hood". Where a
   head appears (items 08/14/15/19), it is a pair of little horns at the height of the rump. The photograph shows the head and
   neck towering well above the back, the forequarters carved in the round, and dense bead, rosette and hair carving over the
   neck and body. The render has a few bead rows and dot clusters pasted onto an otherwise smooth, slate-grey body. In 01/12 a
   slanted wedge occludes the whole forepart.
3. **E colossi (Gate front).** At the one distance given (13/22) they are recognisable as human-headed bulls: tall cap, long
   beard, frontal forelegs. But they are small and stiff, and they stand in a narrow doorway set in a vast untextured-looking
   wall. The photo's colossi fill the jamb nearly to the top. There is no close view of an E colossus, so its face, beard,
   wings and feather carving cannot be judged at all.
4. **Composite capitals in situ (04/17, 07/11).** Seen from below at the player's eye, the whole capital assembly (bell,
   volute member, protome) is an unreadable white clutter of boxes and a "saucer" collar. None of the reference's stacked
   rosette-ended volute pairs or palm-leaf bell can be made out. The shafts read nearly smooth; the reference shaft is finely
   fluted.
5. **Volute member, calibration (16/21).** 21 has plain Ionic-like low-poly spirals (wrong motif; the reference has rosettes on
   the scroll ends). 16 has the right motif (petal rosettes on the scroll ends, flutes between), but it has **visible mesh
   tearing**: ragged rims, jagged slivers along the flutes, and petals broken into shards.
6. **Surface and material.** All the stone reads as the same clean, even, grey-beige CG albedo with a faint noise tile. It has
   no tool marks, arrises, micro-shadowing in the carving, polish difference or dust. Even allowing for fresh 467 stone (D-285),
   the carving never catches light the way cut limestone does. The hall capitals are near-white against a dark interior and
   look self-lit (02/10/18/20).
7. **Scene artefacts outside the carving that break every frame:**
   - a thin bright diagonal line (a seam or crack) on the pier/wall in 04/17 (right) and 08/14 (left);
   - flat saturated blue slabs in 07/11 and a flat blue "tile" band over the Gate door in 13/22, which read as placeholders;
   - a flat red carpet rectangle in the Gate;
   - mud walls with one tiled dirt texture that reads as rock or soil;
   - the flat sky and flat ground plane, as briefed.
8. **The paired photo test is not the one the threshold describes.** T-A5 says "render vs site photograph from its solved
   camera". None of the four renders is from the photograph's camera:
   - pair 1: the render is the calibration view, not the photo's three-quarter view;
   - pair 2: the render shows the whole bull, not the photo's lone head;
   - pair 3: the render is square-on at a distance; the photo stands on the walkway;
   - pair 4: the render looks from inside the passage; the photo looks up from the outside corner.

   So the test measured nothing about matched framing. Even so, 100 % accuracy fails the final standard T-A5d (≤ 65 %). The
   interim threshold "≤ 100 %" cannot fail and is therefore vacuous.

## Scope, lens, time, method (clauses 1–7)

- **Anchors:** `REVIEWS/anchors/` does not exist in this tree. I scored without calibration (clause 7, T-R1 not met).
- **Lens and quality:** Items are 70° at 1.6 m eye height, except the three calibration views at 50° off the Terrace. By my
  identification those are items 03/09, 05/06 and 16/21; they are not places in the game. **All frames are Q=high, not
  `ultra`**, so none is at the player's top quality (clause 2).
- **Scene content:** No terrain, sky model, weather, people, animals or sound. I judged stills only (all 22), so pop, shimmer
  and sound are unjudged (clause 3).
- **Time:** No month or season is modelled. The hour band is late morning (one SE sun plus a hemisphere light) and the
  weather is clear. Every other hour band, every month and every weather is unjudged: dawn and dusk raking light, which is
  exactly what carving needs, as well as night fire-light and overcast.
- **Scope:** The sample covers these places:
  - the Gate passage looking at the W bulls' rumps (both sides);
  - the Gate's E front at about 30 m;
  - two hypostyle interiors looking up (one with black stone door frames, likely Tachara or the Harem; one larger, likely the
    Apadana);
  - calibration pieces.

  It cannot tell you about:
  - the W bulls' heads and forequarters seen from the W stair approach, the principal view;
  - the E colossi close up;
  - the Apadana porticoes in full sun;
  - capitals at mid-distance from outside;
  - the Hundred-Column Hall, the Tripylon or other halls' capitals (e.g. lion or griffin types if modelled);
  - any capital at eye level, e.g. fallen or under construction.
- **References judged against:**
  - `photo_bull_capital_fallen_34.jpg`: protome and saddle.
  - `photo_bull_capital_head_side.jpg`: head, mane, eye, ear.
  - `photo_gate_W_bull_passage.jpg`: W bull.
  - `photo_gate_E_lamassu_front.jpg`: E colossi and Gate proportion.
  - `photo_gate_composite_column.jpg`: volute member, rosettes, bell, fluting.

  I did not consult `handoff/review_briefs.md` or `references/INDEX.md` beyond these supplied five. No memory-only (C)

curl-locks, mane strokes, rosette volute ends). The other is smoother or plainer. The ornamented drawing is always the closer
of the two. **Neither fixes the form** (head, eye, mane, legs, proportion), and form is what fails.

## Overall

- **Capitals: 2/5.** The motif set is roughly right: double protome with saddle, a volute member with rosette ends. But the
  head is a cartoon cow without an eye or mane. In situ the capitals are illegible white clutter, and one drawing tears.
- **Colossi: 1/5.**
  - W bulls: wrong form (head at back height, body boxed in a recess) and almost no carving.
  - E colossi: recognisable only at distance, and never shown close.

## Proxy hunt and detector escapes (clause 8)

- **No PASS was given, so no pass to attack.** One note still applies: had anything passed on the richer drawing's ornament
  alone, the intent would still fail. Ornament density can rise while the head, eye and proportion stay wrong. A detail or
  edge-density metric would reward exactly that.
- **Escapes (for the agent to file, REVIEWS/escapes.md, T-R0):**
  - **Missing-eye and toy-proportion head:** no detector compares protome silhouette or landmarks with the reference photos.
    This is a detector escape if any carving "form vs reference" check is claimed on the board.
  - **The bright diagonal seam or line in 04/17 and 08/14:** a crack or T-junction artefact. A seam or line detector, or the
    T-A3 clip check, should have caught it. This is an escape if either is claimed to run on these views.
  - **Mesh tearing on the rosette volute member (16):** a mesh-validity check on imported carving (degenerate or
    self-intersecting faces) should have caught it.
  - **Flat blue slabs (07/11) and the flat blue band (13/22):** if these are placeholders, T-A1 (placeholder pixels) should
    flag them; if they are tiered glazed brick, they need the glaze material, not a flat colour.
  - **The T-A5 pairs are not camera-matched,** contrary to the threshold's own definition: a process escape.
  - **The W bull head at rump height:** a scale or landmark check against SITE_SPEC (colossus height and head elevation) should
    catch it.
- **New issues (prose only, no Q/B numbers):**
  1. Remodel the protome head from the references: a lidded eye; mane scale-rows over the crown and neck; the curled forelock
     between the horns; the ear form; folded forelegs with knees and hooves.
  2. Rebuild the W bulls: forequarters in the round projecting from the jamb face; head and neck high, near the top of the
     jamb; full body carving.
  3. Scale the E colossi to fill the jamb, and supply close views.
  4. Make the in-situ composite capital legible from the floor. It needs silhouette and value separation, not just ornament.
  5. Fine fluting on the shafts.
  6. Fix the seam line, the mesh tearing and the blue flat slabs.
  7. A carving surface that shows cut-stone light response: micro-occlusion in the recesses, and tool marks on fresh stone.
  8. Re-render the T-A5 pairs from the photographs' solved cameras.
  9. Judge at `ultra`, with terrain and sky, at dawn or dusk raking light.
