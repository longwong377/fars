# Gap audit under D-207: fill or keep out (session 7)

This audit lists every gap in the 467 world that I found. A gap is something left out, drawn as a placeholder, or "not shown because not attested". For each gap it recommends one of two things. **FILL** means build the most probable reconstruction (tier C, labelled in F3 and the translation layer, with its analogy named). **KEEP OUT** means the evidence says the thing was not there. The audit covered DECISIONS.md, PROGRESS.md, BLOCKERS.md, TASKS.md, research/*.md, src/data/*.json and the comments in src/. It also asked, as a historian would, what a town of about 50,000–80,000 people in 467 must have had, and checked the code and data for each of those things.

**How to read it.** "Now" says what the world does today and where (file:line or decision). The evidence column uses three terms. *Attested* means a source for Persepolis or the Achaemenid court says it. *Silent* means no source either way. *Against* means a source says it was not there. A citation marked **(R)** is my recollection and is **NOT SEEN** in this session (the scholarly hosts are blocked, B6). Check it before tiering anything above C. Effort: S is under a day of agent work, M is one to three days, L is more. Workstreams: sim, settlement, people look, sound, architecture, religion.

## 2. What must stay out, and why

**Evidence against:**
- **Fire temples and any temple building** in 467. Herodotus 1.131 says the Persians set up no temples or altars in the Greek sense and worship on the heights. No fire temple is excavated at Persepolis. Temple cult dates from Artaxerxes II and the domed fire temple is Sasanian (blocklist `sasanian-fire-temple`). The fill is the open-air fire cult of item 1. The "Frataraka" pedestal temple is post-Achaemenid (blocklist `frataraka`).
- **Images of gods in Persian hands**, whether statues, household idols or figurines (Herodotus 1.131). Foreigners' own amulets and figurines are allowed (item 14).
- **Music at a sacrifice:** "no pipes" (Herodotus 1.132, M-05). Only the voice sounds.
- **Fire at the Herodotean sacrifice itself.** Herodotus 1.132 says no fire is kindled for it, so the sacrifice stays fireless. Fire belongs to the separate fire cult, which rests on the Naqsh-e Rustam relief and the Pasargadae plinths.
- **A market square with coins.** Herodotus 1.153 says the Persians have no market places (R, B claim), and everyday coin is blocklisted (`coins-everyday`, Q-011). Exchange in kind, hawkers and weighed silver among foreign traders are the fill.
- **Anything later than 467:** Sasanian reliefs and inscriptions, the later tombs at Naqsh-e Rustam (Artaxerxes I, Darius II), Palace H, the Artaxerxes III W stair of the Tachara, the Spring Cemetery, Istakhr, the later sites on the plain, Band-e Amir and the modern dams.
- **Anachronistic objects and species:** stirrups, horseshoes, candles, glass window panes, paper, date palms on the frosty plain, rice, cotton, citrus and New World crops, turkeys and water buffalo. The domestic cat as a pet stays out: it is uncertain, and absence is the more probable state.
- **Qanats.** None is dated to the Achaemenid period (Q-052), and the plain was watered from its rivers and canals. Absence is the most probable state here. Revisit only if a dated Achaemenid qanat is found.

**Out by rule, not by evidence** (the user may lift these; D-207 keeps them):
- **Invented words:** no invented liturgy (D-207), no invented inscription text (the 6 lost stretches of DNb stay uncut; brief §10), and no invented names (brief §9.1).
- **Showing exposure of the dead.** Herodotus 1.140 says the magi did it, "secretly". It stays off screen (brief §12, not sensational), but the magi's dead may be carried up the mountain.
- **Combat, arrest or visible punishment.** Brief §9.1 forbids combat, and D-103 governs the visitor's stop.
- **The king in the default world.** Brief §2 binds: he is present only on dates the evidence supports (Q-005, B9). The court setting is the fill.
- **Hollywood tropes:** harem clichés, bare-chested slave soldiers, gold on everything, Greek hoplite gear on Persians.
