# D-307 people seen close, round 2: blind review (session 11)

Reviewer: independent, blind; did not build what is judged. Brief: handoff/briefs/s11/review_d307_people_2.md.

## Lead: what is broken

**Every one of the 30 items reads as CG (30/30). No item reaches 4 in any category; T-A4 (lowest category >= 4) and T-A4cg
(0 read as CG) both FAIL on every item.** The sample does not contain a single frame a viewer would take for a photograph of a
person. The best items (plain faces of workers, women and children) reach "good game NPC", not "photograph".

What breaks it, in the order a person standing 1.5 m away would notice:

1. **Beards and hair are geometry shells or flat cards.** The court beards are either a stack of 3-4 rubber rings with a spiral
   decal printed on each (the carved relief convention copied literally into 3D: P01, P09, P17, P22, P28), or a slab of
   parallel strip-cards with a hard, square-cut lower edge and stair-stepped alpha (P05, P08, P19, P23, P25, P30). Women's hair
   is a solid bob helmet (P07, P24) or a handful of spiky alpha cards with aliasing (P03, P15, P29). Men's scalp hair is a
   stippled skullcap. Brows are thin drawn lines. Nothing has strand-level softness, flyaways or translucency.
2. **Belts float.** Every belt is a rigid cylindrical hoop standing off the body with a visible gap and a dark interior (P03,
   P07, P10, P15, P24 most clearly; present on nearly all). No cinching, no gathered cloth above or below it.
3. **Cloth has no weight or construction.** Tunics are smooth shrink-wrapped shells; the "pleats" of the court robe are a
   vertical stripe texture that aliases into moire and smears (P03, P07, P12, P15, P24, P30), or a streaked blur below the belt
   (P01, P05, P08, P09, P17, P19, P22, P23). Bell sleeves are single-sided sheets with black interiors (P01, P05, P08, P17,
   P19, P22). The Median kandys' empty sleeves hang as two stiff dark planks that read as a second pair of arms (P25, P28).
   Child tunics read as a modern T-shirt plus a separate flared skirt with a seam gap at the waist (P11, P18, P21, P26).
4. **Headgear is primitive geometry.** Fluted caps are plain cylinders with a normal-map ripple; the crenellated crown (P06,
   P29) is a crumpled gold band with a white opaque veil plane behind the head that intersects the shoulders; worker fillets
   are floating discs (P02, P13, P14, P27); the women's hood (P10, P20) is a rubbery shell with a hard, glued hem.
5. **Skin, hands and pose.** Skin is uniform, waxy (fire items) or matte-plastic (day items), with no pores, redness,
   sweat or dust; faces are symmetrical and repeated across classes. Every figure stands in the same near-A-pose with clawed
   hands and dark smudged fingertips/nail artefacts (P01, P04, P08, P12, P13). Shadow-map stair-stepping lies across faces and
   chests in every day item (P15 worst: blotches across the face).

Uncalibrated: **no anchor set exists** (REVIEWS/anchors/ absent), so these scores are not tied to a pinned scale (clause 7).

- **Scope.** A probe page (humanlab.html): a plain ground and one wall, no town. It samples the close-range look of every class
  shown, but says nothing about people in real scenes (Terrace light, interiors, crowds, grime from actual work) or at other
  distances.
- **Lens.** Frames stated as the default 60 deg lens at quality **high**, not `ultra`; per clause 2 every frame here is **not at
  the widest quality setting**. Crops are 2x nearest-neighbour, which exaggerates aliasing; I judged failures that are also
  visible in the full frames (checked P01, P05, P06, P11, P19, P25 frames).
- **Motion and sound.** Stills only; all 30 items judged as stills. Nothing can be said about gait, idle motion, cloth motion,
  lip-sync, hair motion or the mute/spoken crowd.
- **Time.** Day items: day 25 (late spring), 10:00, clear. Fire items: same day 22:00, clear, brazier 1.3 m right. Unjudged:
  every other hour band (dawn, dusk, noon glare, interior shade), every other season, and all weather (wind, rain, dust, cold
  clothing).
- **Scene (clause 5).** There is no scene: no place, no activity, no sound; people stand still facing the camera. Judged as a
  person met in conversation, not as a scene.

## Top five breakers

1. **Beards and hair as rings, cards or helmets** (ring-stack spiral beards; square-cut strip-card beards; solid bob helmets;
   aliased spiky cards). The single worst element, on every court man and every woman.
2. **Floating hoop belts** with a visible gap and open interior, on almost every item.
3. **Cloth without weight**: shrink-wrapped tunics, striped-texture pleats that alias/smear, single-sided bell sleeves with black
   interiors, kandys sleeves as rigid planks.
4. **Primitive headgear**: cylinder caps, floating fillet discs, crumpled crown with an opaque veil plane intersecting the body,
   rubbery hoods.
5. **Mannequin bodies**: waxy/plastic, pore-less, un-dirtied skin on a few repeated faces; identical A-pose with clawed hands
   and dark fingertip artefacts; hard, stair-stepped shadow maps across faces and chests.

## Anachronism and identifiability (T-I1)

- No modern object seen. Dress broadly follows the reliefs (court robe with fluted cap, Median hood and kandys, earrings,
  bangles). But:
  - the ring-stack beard is the **stone carving convention** turned into an object; a real 467 beard was hair, curled with
    tongs, not rigid tiers (misreading of the reference, not an anachronism).
  - children's dress reads as a **modern T-shirt and skirt** at a glance.
  - the crowned figures' crown + flat white veil plane does not read as any attested form; if it is meant as the king or a
    court woman, it is not identifiable as either.
- **Identifiability**: court men are identifiable as "Persian court" by cap and robe; Median dress is identifiable by hood and
  kandys; workers, women and children are generic. **No figure is identifiable as the king**; no guard carries a spear, bow or
  quiver, so **guards are not identifiable** in this sample; no delegation dress is recognisable. The same faces recur across
  classes (low physical variety for a cosmopolitan centre).

## Proxy hunt and detector escapes (clause 8)

- No PASS was given, so no pass-while-intent-fails question arises.
- Floating belts (gap between belt and torso), single-sided sleeves rendering black, and the crown's veil plane intersecting the
  shoulders are geometric faults a mesh-clearance / backface / interpenetration detector should catch: **detector escapes**
  if the board had such detectors and they passed.
- Pleat-stripe moire and stair-stepped shadow maps are measurable (high-frequency aliasing energy on the garment region;
  shadow-edge staircase metric): detector escapes if a shimmer/alias detector exists and passed these.
- Card beards and helmet hair, waxy skin and pose repetition are judgement failures a detector would not reliably catch.
